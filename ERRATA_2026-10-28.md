## E5. Rate-law gap: "within-step dependence of R on B" is ruled out

Applies to: `AI Agent Audits/AIEFS_audit_response_3.md` §2.2 (line 62), which lists "a within-step dependence of R on B,
which would violate (c)" as an unverified candidate source of the ~2e-8 rate-law gap. Ledger row: `AUD-RATELAW`.
Raised by the Arena AI review of `d041c69`; checked here against the engine code.

In `aief_experiment_engine_v1.py`, `advance_state` runs in this order:

1. `:779` `R_physical = compute_R(state, config)`. `compute_R` (`:721-752`) reads only `state.theta`, `state.history[0]`
   and the EMA `state.coherence_acc` (`:730-745`), updates the EMA in place (`:743-745`) and returns `|EMA|` symmetrised.
   It never reads `state.B`.
2. `:790-797` the proposal `B + dt((η+μ)R − μ − γB)`, clipped at 0 and symmetrised, uses that already-computed `R`.
3. `:803-813` the capacity gate. `:823` `state.B = B_projected`.
4. `:830-838` θ is updated with the new `B`. `:842-844` history is updated after that.

So a perturbation of `B` alone reaches the phase only through step 4, after `R` has been computed for this step. It
can affect `R` from the next step on, but not the `R` used in the step's own `B` update. The audit's own driver says
the same (`AI Agent Audits/evidence/scripts/kappa_cycle_kernel.py:10`, "R does not depend on B within a step"), and
its `step_B` (`:52-57`) measures `B'` after one `advance_state` call. Condition (c) of the rate law therefore holds
automatically for the B-only perturbations used in the census, and this candidate is **ruled out**.

The remaining candidates (none verified or isolated) are: finite-difference error at the per-component δ, unequal
controlling scales (the max spread of `s` on J is 8.5e-10), finite-time departure from the ideal locked/saturated
point, and storage rounding in the audit result files. The audit file is hash-pinned and is not edited.

Residual split (Auditor Seat 002 re-check at 886daf6, `RECHECK.md` §9.2): the 2.04e-8 maximum comes from a single component
whose finite-difference step δ lies in [1e-9, 1e-8). The other 263 well-resolved components (δ ≥ 1e-6) agree within
2.1e-10 (median 6.3e-13), and error × δ is roughly constant (median ~6e-18), as expected for rounding that scales as 1/δ.
Finite-difference/rounding error is therefore the most likely cause. Finite-time departure is less likely (response 3
reports the same gap against the state formula as against the closed form), and so are unequal controlling scales (a spread
of 8.5e-10 is too small to give 2e-8). The gap stays labelled **unexplained until a step-size sweep is run**.
